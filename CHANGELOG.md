# Changes

## Unreleased

The first version.

### New

- `playSound("coin")`: fourteen ready-made game sounds (coin, jump, laser, shoot, explosion, hit, powerup, blip, click, zap, bounce, pop, win, lose), each with `{ volume, pitch, pan }`.
- `playNote("C4")`, `playNote(["C4", "E4", "G4"], 1, 0.5)`: notes and chords by name or number, with a length and a loudness.
- `playNotes("C4 E4 G4 - C5:2", 0.25, { repeat: true })`: a tune, kept in time by the sound engine itself.
- `makeSynth("bass")`, `makeSynth({ wave: "saw", cutoff: 1200 })`: nineteen ready-made synths and drums, and synths made from settings. Every setting is a plain property (`lead.cutoff = 800`), under a friendly name or the engine's own.
- Chords by name wherever a note is played: `lead.play("Cm7")`, `playNotes("Am F C G", 0.5)`, `chordNotes("Fmaj7")`.
- Arpeggios, the fast chords of a C64 tune, from one voice: `playArp("Cm7")`, `lead.arp("C4", "minor", 2, { speed: 50, order: "up-down" })`, and `startArp("Am")` for one that stays and changes chord with `chord.change("F")` without starting again.
- Step macros for chip sounds: `makeSynth("chip", { macro: [{ wave: "noise", pitch: 24 }, { wave: "square" }] })`, `lead.macro = [...]`. The chip drums, the chip bass and the sounds hit, shoot and powerup are made of them.
- A synth is something [dials.js](https://github.com/johnwylie70/dials.js) can be tied to as it is: `dial("cutoff", lead, "cutoff")`, `scope(lead)`, `meter(lead)`, `keyboard(lead)`. For your own drawing: `lead.samples()`, `lead.level()`, `lead.notes()`, and `soundOutput()` for all the sound.
- Note lengths by name, measured against `setTempo`: `playNote("C4", "quarter")`, `"eighth."`, `"1/16"`, `playSound("win", { length: "half" })`. A number is still seconds.
- A tune as ordinary code, one note a line, each beginning where the last ended: `note("E4", "quarter")`, `rest("eighth")`, `chord("Cm", "whole")`, on any synth (`lead.note`), lines written together start together, `loopNotes(() => { … })` has a section go round, `startLines()` begins again.
- In `playNotes`, lengths on notes (`"C4:q E4:e G4:h"`) and a step by name (`"eighth"`); a tune in beats changes speed when `setTempo` is called.
- `lead.start("C4")` gives a note that stays, and can be slid, turned down and stopped.
- Effects in one word: `lead.echo`, `lead.reverb`, `lead.chorus`, `lead.distortion`, `lead.crush`, and `setEffects({ echoTime: 0.3 })` for the ones every synth shares.
- `loadSample("kick.wav")`: a recorded sound, played like any synth.
- `{ after }`, `{ at }` and `{ beat }` on everything that makes a sound, with `setTempo`, `beats` and `soundTime`. Sounds land on the exact sample.
- `setVolume`, `muteSound`, `unmuteSound`, `stopSound`.
- `playSong("tune.mod")`: tracker songs (`.mod`, `.xm`) and songs in synths' own readable format, played by the engine itself, with `pause`, `resume`, `seek`, `volume`, `stop` and `position`, and `{ patches }` to say what a song's named synths are.
- Several scenes on one page share one sound card: each `createSound()` stops, mutes and pauses alone.
- MIDI. `lead.playFromMidi()`: a MIDI keyboard plays a synth, with how hard each key is hit, the pitch and modulation wheels and the sustain pedal. `onMidiNote`, `onMidiNoteOff`, `onMidiControl` and `onMidi` call your functions with what arrives. `sendMidiNote("C4", 0.8, { length: 0.5, channel: 1 })`, `sendMidiControl`, `sendMidiBend`, `sendMidiProgram` and `sendMidi(bytes)` send, at the exact time `{ after, at, beat }` asks for; `lead.sendToMidi()` sends what a synth plays; `sendMidiClock()` sends the beat. `lead.learn("cutoff")` and `lead.mapMidi(74, "cutoff")` tie a knob to a setting through the setting's own range. `midiDevices`, `onMidiDevices`, `useMidiInput`, `useMidiOutput`, `stopMidi`. It is in a file of its own, `synths-midi.js`, fetched only when MIDI is used, which is also when the browser asks its permission. Where a browser has no MIDI (Safari, iPhones, iPads) everything still runs and the console says so once.
- The reverb has types. `reverbType` is `"room"` (the one to begin with), `"hall"`, `"plate"`, `"cathedral"` or `"spring"`; `reverbDecay` is how many seconds it rings, `reverbPredelay` a gap before it begins and `reverbLowCut` keeps the bass out of it. There is one reverb, which every synth sends into: `lead.reverbType = "hall"`, `setEffects({ reverbType: "hall" })` and a control tied to any synth or to `soundOutput()` all set the same thing, and every synth reads the same value back (before, each synth remembered its own for `reverbSize` and the other shared settings). The room to begin with is smaller than it was: its tail is about a second where it was a second and a half, and `reverbSize` begins at 0.5. No ready-made synth chooses a type, since that would change it for the others; the pad, the strings and the bell were measured in the new room (their notes end within a third of a second of where they did, as loud as they were to within about a decibel) and sound grander in a `"hall"`. The voice `cave` is a cathedral now, and `monster` a little wetter.
- The MIDI device and channel can be chosen on the screen. `midiInput()` is something a control of dials.js is tied to: `select("midi in", midiInput(), "device")`, `select("channel", midiInput(), "channel")` and `meter(midiInput())` for a light that flashes with the keys. The choices follow the devices as they are plugged in and pulled out, the chosen device is remembered for next time, and where there is no MIDI the one choice says so. `lead.midiInput()` is a synth's own, so that two synths on two channels split a keyboard, and `midiOutput()` and `lead.midiOutput()` are the same for what is sent. In code: `midiIn.device = "Keystation 49"`, `midiIn.channel = 2`, `midiIn.level()`, `midiIn.notes()`, `midiIn.onChange(...)`.
- `useMidiInput` and `useMidiOutput` are each program's own choice and no longer the whole page's, and a device that is not plugged in no longer means silence: every input is listened to (or the first output sent to) until it appears, and the console says so once. `lead.playFromMidi()` and `lead.sendToMidi()` keep the channel they were last given; `playFromMidi({ channel: "all" })` is every channel again.
- The microphone. `const mic = useMicrophone()` asks for it (only then), and it is a synth whose sound comes in: `mic.voice = "robot"` (robot, chipmunk, monster, cave, telephone, alien, whisper), `mic.listen()` and `mic.mute()`, `mic.pitch`, `mic.ring`, `mic.comb`, `mic.gain`, `mic.gate` and every setting a synth has, and `dial("pitch", mic, "pitch")`, `scope(mic)`, `meter(mic)` with dials.js. It is not heard until `listen()`, and starts with a gentle gate.
- Recording. `recordSound(3)`, `startRecording()` and `stopRecording()` record the microphone into a sample that is given back at once (`take.done`, `take.seconds`, `take.onDone`, `take.whenDone()`), plays like any sample (`take.play("E4")`, `take.reverse`, `take.loop`) and wears the microphone's voice (`take.voice`, `take.soundLike(mic)`). `recordOutput(10)` records everything that is played, and `lead.record(4)` one synth. `saveSound(take, "hello.wav")` saves a WAV file.
- The voice effects on every synth: `lead.pitchShift`, `lead.ringMod`, `lead.ringSpeed`, `lead.comb`, `lead.combTone`, `lead.flanger`, and `lead.voice = "robot"`.
- With no microphone (none plugged in, refused, a page that may not ask) nothing throws: one message says why and what to do, and a recording is empty.
- For apps, the microphone travels as the sound does: `mic`, `record` and `save` commands, what the player says back as plain data (`player.onSoundEvents`, `sound.receiveSoundEvents`), a `microphone` option for a host that supplies a stream or pushes the frames, a `save` option for the files, and sound in a microphone's place for the offline player ([docs/microphone.md](docs/microphone.md)).
- Three more examples: Voice changer, Sampler, Record a tune.
- Songs, for an editor: `song.mute(channel)`, `song.solo(channel)`, `song.loopPattern()`, and `song.replace(edited)` to put an edited song in the place of the one playing without stopping it. A song can say itself what its synth instruments are (`synth: { preset, set }`), and can carry its recordings inside itself.
- `lead.set({ cutoff: 4000 }, { glide: 2 })` also understands `slide`.
- Any synth can have a sample for its wave: `lead.sample = loadSample("piano.wav")`, or `makeSynth({ sample: "piano.wav", note: "C4" })`. The wave `"sample"` is offered only on a synth that has one, and setting it on one that has none says how to give it one and keeps its wave.
- Two more settings by a friendly name: `pwm` and `pwmRate`, the pulse wave's width moving in and out and how fast (the same slow wobbler as `wobble`, so `pwmRate` is `wobbleRate`).
- For apps, MIDI travels as the sound does: a `midi` command in the same batches ([docs/midi.md](docs/midi.md)), carried out beside the player, with what is received handed back as plain data (`player.onMidi`, `sound.receiveMidi`, `sound.useMidiHooks(scene)`), and a `midi` option for an app that reaches the devices its own way.
- Every function in its Python spelling too (`play_sound`, `make_synth`).
- Mistakes said in plain words, with "Did you mean".
- Sound waits for the browser's first tap by itself, and says once that it is waiting.
- For apps: the functions write commands ([docs/commands.md](docs/commands.md)) that a `sound` option can take and a player elsewhere can play; the engine as bytes and the worklet from a Blob, for a page that may fetch nothing; an offline player that renders the same commands to samples or a WAV file, in a browser or in node; and a fallback for a browser with no audio worklet.

- `setSoundBuffer("large")` and `soundBuffer()` (`set_sound_buffer`, `sound_buffer`): how much sound is made at a time, set in a program's own code and read back as `{ frames, delay, thread, underruns, setting }`. `"small"`, `"medium"`, `"large"`, `"auto"` or a number of frames. On a page with no https address it is the buffer itself (larger for fewer clicks, smaller for a keyboard that feels late); on the audio thread, where the buffer cannot change, it sets how far ahead everything is scheduled, and says so once. It travels as a `buffer` command, so Python and a scene in a frame can send it.

- `describe(name)` gives `labels` for values that mean something else, for a dial to show: `reverbDecay` 0 is `"auto"`, the microphone's `gate` at -100 is `"off"`. The table of settings in `synths-names.json` carries them.

- `loadSamples({ C2: "piano-C2.wav", C3: "piano-C3.wav" })` (`load_samples`): several recordings as one instrument, each playing the keys nearest the note it was played at. The notes can be read from the files' names (`loadSamples(["piano-C2.wav", "piano-Fs2.wav"])`); `{ kit: true }` is a drum kit; `{ crossfade: 3 }` fades neighbours together; a list of `{ file, note, from, to, loud, tune, volume, release }` gives layers by loudness and everything by hand; several files for one note go in turn. On an ordinary synth: `lead.samples = { … }`, `makeSynth({ samples })`. `piano.zones()` reads it back, and a song's instrument can be given one. It travels as a `zones` command. `examples/piano.js` is a piano of nine real recordings (University of Iowa Musical Instrument Samples).
- The engine is synths-core a0b6535, which has sample maps.
- `describe(name).aliases`: the other words a choice may be set by (`{ square: "pulse", sawtooth: "saw" }`), for a control that reads a choice back by name.

- For apps: `createPlayer({ core, context })` takes an engine the app runs itself (a native app's, on its own audio thread) and the app's clock, and then fetches no engine and uses no Web Audio. [docs/core.md](docs/core.md) is the contract: every op the player sends a core, what a core says back, and the timing rule.

- Musical arpeggios beside the fast ones: `lead.arp("Am", "whole", { step: "sixteenth", octaves: 2, gate: 0.5 })` strikes a new note at each step, in time with `setTempo` (and follows it while it sounds). `gate`, `octaves`, `swing`, `accent`, a `pattern` (`"1 - 3 2 _ 1 3 5^"` or `"trance"`), the orders `"up-down2"` and `"together"`, and `style: "slide"` for one voice. `chord.change("F")` on one that is held keeps its place. In a `note` command's `arp` the fields are `beats`, `style`, `gate`, `octaves`, `swing`, `accent` and `pattern`, beside `offsets`, `rate` and `order`. `playArp("Cm7")` with nothing said is the C64 chord it always was.

- The trance gate: `pad.gate("x.x.xx.x")` chops a synth's sound into a pattern of steps in time with the tempo, by its steps, by name (`"trance"`, `"pump"`) or as a list of levels, with `{ rate, depth, smooth, swing, stereo, sends }`; `pad.gate("off")`. Its parts are settings for dials (`gateDepth`, `gateRate`, `gateSmooth`, `gateSwing`) and `pad.gatePattern`.
- `lead.arpPattern`: a synth's own pattern for its arpeggios, set as text, a name or a list and read back as text, taken by the arpeggio that is sounding at its next step. `lead.arpStep()` and `pad.gateStep()` are the step that is sounding, for an editor to light.

- A note's pitch can move: `lead.play("A4", 1, { bend: "scoop" })`, `"fall"`, `"dip"`, `{ to: 2, over: "eighth" }`; `{ glide: "sixteenth" }` slides to the note from the synth's last one; `{ slide: 12 }`; `{ vibrato: true }` or `{ depth, rate, delay }`, and `note.vibrato()` on a note that is held. In `play`, `start` and `playNote`.
- `lead.noteOn("C4", 0.8, { at })` and `lead.noteOff("C4", { at })` take a time of `soundTime()`, exact to the sample, for a sequencer that looks ahead.

- A song's mix, moved while it plays: `song.channel(n).volume`, `.pan`, `.reverb` and `.echo`, and `song.loop([1, 0], [5, 0])` for a stretch that goes round. `reverbLevel` and `echoLevel` are new settings for how loud the shared reverb and echo come back. A song's own `mixer`, `effects` and `loop` are handed to the engine as they are; the effects a song set are put back when it goes.

### Fixed

- `song.channel(n).level()` read zero for a channel whose instrument is a synth patch. It is now what is heard on the channel whichever kind of instrument plays it (the engine, synths-core a0b6535, shows a channel's synth notes on the channel's own meter).
- Inside an app whose web view patches `fetch` (Capacitor's HTTP plugin), the engine's file could come back as "ok" with nothing in it, and the sound failed with WebAssembly's own words ("doesn't parse at byte 0"). What is fetched is now looked at before it is believed (the engine has to be a WebAssembly module, a sample or a song not empty), and an empty, wrong or failed reply is asked for again: through the fetch a host kept from before the patch, through `XMLHttpRequest`, and once more past the cache. If every way gives nothing, the reason says so: "the engine's file came back empty from …".
- `voice` is described as a choice, and a control that sets a choice by its place in the list (a select, a rotary switch) made it throw. A voice may now be set by its place or its name, on the microphone, a synth and a player's target; a target describes and reads `voice` too.
- A scene whose first line is the microphone (`useMicrophone()` with no note played) now says once that sound is waiting for a tap, as any other first sound does.
- On a page with no audio worklet (one not served over https or from localhost, such as a development build opened on an iPad) the sound stuttered and clicked whenever the page was busy for more than about 20 ms. The fallback now makes 2048 frames at a time where it made 1024, which rides over 30 ms; a key is heard about 95 ms after it is pressed where it was 80. `fallbackBuffer` sets it (`4096` for a busier page, heard about 190 ms late), `"auto"` doubles it once if the page keeps being too busy, and `player.fallback` is `{ buffer, underruns, reason }`. The table of what was measured is in [docs/commands.md](docs/commands.md).
- On the same fallback, a callback that came late (but in time) moved the notes inside its buffer by as much as it was late. The engine's clock now goes by the buffers' own grid, and scheduled notes are exact to the sample while the page is busy.
- What was asked for while the engine was still loading (a repeating tune, `loopNotes`, a song) could stay silent for as long again as the page had been open: always on the fallback with no audio worklet, and now and then in WebKit. It now starts as soon as the engine is there.
- A song that does not repeat lets its last notes go at its end.
